# engine

Consumes `odds.delta` with consumer group `engine` (consumer name from `ENGINE_CONSUMER`, else `engine-<hostname>`). On start, and every 5 minutes after, it drops consumers of the group that have been idle for 10 minutes (`XGROUP DELCONSUMER`). These are the names of earlier containers, so a restart under a new hostname would otherwise leave their pending entries stuck forever. Their prices are restored from `prices_current` and re-polled by ingest. It then re-reads its own pending entries (cursor `0`) and switches to `>`. One goroutine reads up to 2048 entries per `XREADGROUP` and prefetches up to 4 reads ahead of a second goroutine that decodes and routes them, so the read round trip overlaps routing. Each event is pinned to one shard on first sight, choosing the shard with the fewest events (hashing put 5 of 40 events on one of 16 shards). That goroutine owns every market of the event, so the board is never locked.

A shard takes whatever is queued in its inbox (up to 512 deltas), applies them one by one in arrival order, and passes the batch to its own writer goroutine. The writer sends one Redis pipeline for the batch and then acks. While the writer waits on Redis, the shard applies the next batch. Writes for one shard stay FIFO, so per-event order is kept.

Per delta the shard does the following:
1. Drops selections whose side does not fit the market's shape (`pricing.FitsShape`, for example a draw on a basketball `ML` from a 3-way regulation line), counted in `engine_misshapen_cells_dropped_total{source,code}`. Replaces that source's selections for the market and de-vigs them (`BoardCell.Fair` per source cell). A selection repeated within one delta keeps its first occurrence and the rest are counted in `engine_duplicate_cells_dropped_total{source}`. A source-market over `pricing.maxOverround` is flagged and left out of consensus, arbs and +EV. When a source's `Delta.Market.SourceKey` changes for the same market key (two source markets colliding on one canonical key), that source's move history is reset so the two are never compared.
2. Recomputes computed markets. Full-time 1X2 derives DC and DNB for that source (`Derived=true`, never an arb leg, skipped when the source quotes the real market). A `T_*` market without a line whose labels parse as buckets becomes ladder rungs (`T_* | line e-0.5`). Rungs are not marked derived, because they can be bet as a bundle of buckets.
3. Rescans only the touched market keys: consensus cells (`source=consensus`, `Fair` set), arbs, +EV, moves, and PrizePicks line gaps for the line family.
4. Hands price rows to the flusher and signal changes to the signal publisher. Both are mutex-guarded queues, so a slow Postgres never blocks a shard.

Per batch the writer merges the change sets by event. For each board field the last write in the batch wins: a cell removed and then re-added is `HSET`, and one added and then removed is `HDEL`. One `HSET` per market, one `SADD board:event:<id>`, and one `PUBLISH event:<id>` BoardUpdate per event go out in a single pipeline. The BoardUpdate carries every cell change of the batch in apply order, including tombstones (`price.suspended=true`, `prob=0`). The batch's `XACK` (one command, all ids) is sent only after that pipeline succeeds, so a failed write leaves the deltas pending.

Freshness marks (`f`) refresh `observedAt` only on the cells of markets whose source market key the mark lists. When the mark is `Complete`, the source's other markets on that event are suspended, keeping their last price and `observedAt`. The suspended rows are persisted as a raw change, open signals on them close in the same rescan, and `engine_withdrawn_cells_suspended_total{source}` counts them. Suspending is idempotent because already-suspended markets are skipped. Restored markets carry no source key, so marks neither refresh nor suspend them. Their `observedAt` stays at the persisted value, which keeps them out of arbs (`maxLegSkew`), until the source sends the market again. An ingest restart re-sends every market.

Flusher: shards append their row chunks to a queue. Four writers each take up to `flushRows` rows when the queue reaches `flushRows` or `flushEvery` passes. Concurrent batches are safe because every statement is latest-wins or `DO NOTHING`. Each writer `COPY`s into a temp table and, in one transaction with a 30s timeout, runs three statements:
- upsert `prices_current` (latest `observedAt` wins). A row whose values are unchanged is only rewritten once its stored `observedAt` is 10 minutes old, so a re-observed price does not create a dead tuple on every poll.
- insert `prices_history` for rows whose raw/suspended/form changed (`ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, so replays are idempotent)
- upsert `closing_prices` with the last non-suspended observation before `events.kickoff`. It uses the same unchanged-row rule, so its `observedAt` may trail the last identical observation by up to 10 minutes.

Rows whose event or player does not exist are skipped. A failed batch goes back to the front of the queue. The in-memory queue holds up to 64× `flushRows` rows. Overflow is appended to a gob-encoded segment spool on local disk (`ENGINE_SPOOL_DIR`, default `$TMPDIR/mybestbet-spool-<consumer>`; segments of 200k rows), so shards never wait on Postgres and no price change is dropped. Writers drain the oldest sealed segment whenever the in-memory queue is below one batch, delete it only after every batch commits, and keep it for retry on failure. Replays are safe because `prices_current` and `closing_prices` only accept newer observations and history inserts are `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`. On start the engine adopts segments left by a crash; on shutdown unwritten rows are spilled. `engine_price_rows_spilled_total` counts spilled rows; only a failing disk drops rows, logged as `dropping prices the spool could not hold`.

Signals: lifecycle per market (or line family) goes open → updated (re-emitted only when margin/EV/leg prices change) → closed when no longer found. Shards queue changes keyed by (dedupeKey, id), so repeated updates of one signal coalesce to the latest. Every 100 ms the publisher takes the whole queue and runs it in one transaction, in rounds: round k holds the k-th instance of each dedupeKey ordered by close time, with the open one last. Each round runs one multi-row upsert on `("dedupeKey") WHERE status='open'` (`peakMargin` keeps the max; ids already closed are skipped) and then one multi-row close (`status='open'` and `detectedAt <= closedAt`, so a later reopen is never closed by an older close). A signal that opened and closed inside one window still gets its row and both emissions. After commit, one pipeline does `XADD signals` and `PUBLISH signals.live` per signal and `PUBLISH event:<id>` (BoardUpdate with `signals`) per event. Closing an already closed signal emits nothing. A failed transaction is requeued unless a newer state of that signal arrived meanwhile.

Restart: open signals and `prices_current` (events kicking off after now-6h, status scheduled/live) are loaded into the shards. Every event is rescanned and rewritten to Redis, and open signals with no market left are closed. Restored prices do not seed move history (they carry no source market key and may be stale), so every open move closes on restart and moves restart from live observations.

Kickoff: every minute the engine closes open signals whose event has kicked off (in-play is out of scope and ingest stops polling at kickoff, so their legs are frozen). The closes go through the signal publisher, so `signals` and `signals.live` see them.

Partitions: `ensure_prices_history_partition` for today and tomorrow (UTC) runs at start and hourly. Source health (`source:health:<name>` `degraded`) is cached and refreshed every 2s. Degraded sources are left out of consensus and arbs. Consensus and +EV count brands of one feed (`sources.<name>.feed`) once; arbs may still use any brand as a leg.

Board cells copy a price's `bid`/`ask`/`liquidity` pointers on ingest. goccy/go-json allocates decoded pointers from a shared arena, so keeping the decoded pointer in the board or move history would pin that whole arena. A line family is scanned for PrizePicks gaps only when some over cell in it is `line_only`; otherwise its open line signals are reconciled against an empty result.

Known gaps: a multi-bucket ladder leg pays the prediction-market fee once, not once per bucket contract. `VoidIfNoStart`/`Resolver` do not take part in settle-rule matching (only `RegulationOnly` is stored in `prices_current`).

Benchmark (`go test -bench . ./internal/engine/`, M-series laptop): one changed 1X2 market with 15 books, including the DC/DNB rescans, takes ~65 µs/op on an idle machine. Allocation dropped from 228 KB/op to 64 KB/op (324 to 187 allocs) after the quote buffer reuse and the copy-free filters in `internal/signal`. Under the 10x load test the allocation rate (about 2 GB/s) and the move history (up to 512 points per cell, about 0.7 GB live after a minute) cost more CPU than the scans: the runtime spends roughly a third of the engine's CPU zeroing and re-faulting freed heap pages (`memclrNoHeapPointers`, `madvise`).
